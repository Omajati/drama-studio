PROJECT CONTEXT:
Project: a 17-minute English science documentary for adults on the YouTube channel about prehistoric humans, titled "What Did Ice Age People Actually Do All Day? A Child Left Proof". Story: one winter day, hour by hour, in an open-air camp of mammoth hunters about 30,000 years ago at Dolní Věstonice and Pavlov (South Moravia, Czech Republic), on a slope above a river under limestone hills. Every hour has a real clue: hut outlines with hearths sealed by wind-blown loess; toe bones that show they wore shoes (Trinkaus, 2005); a grinding stone with starch of cattail and ferns from Pavlov VI (Revedin et al., PNAS, 2010); fur animals and nets, woven impressions on clay (Adovasio, Soffer, Klíma, 1996); the oldest ceramic workshop with a clay oven and thousands of fired clay figures, many burst in the fire (Vandiver et al., Science, 1989); the Venus of Dolní Věstonice with a child's fingerprint (Králík et al.) and a micro-CT scan showing she was made of local loess from one piece (Neruda et al., 2024); a woman from Isturitz Cave in France with ancestry linked to central and eastern Europe (Gelabert, Pinhasi et al., Science Advances, October 2026); the contested "dog" of Předmostí; the triple burial with red ochre and the woman buried under mammoth shoulder blades. Through-question: did they have any free time? Twist: the first fired clay on Earth was made for figures, not for pots. Opens and ends with the same child at dawn and a fingerprint in clay; teaser for a video about four kinds of humans 40,000 years ago.

Visual style (used for every image): painterly realism, scientifically grounded reconstruction of Upper Paleolithic life (Gravettian culture), warm firelight inside huts, cold blue-white winter daylight outside; documentary photograph look for excavations, museums and labs; stylized Ice Age relief map for routes; clean flat scientific illustration for simple comparisons.

Recurring characters (keep identical across shots):
THE CHILD — a child about nine years old, early modern human, weathered light-brown skin, dark tangled hair, dark curious eyes, a hooded parka of reindeer and fox fur with neat sewn seams, fur boots. Calm and curious, never frightened.
THE WOMAN — an adult woman about thirty, early modern human, strong hands, dark hair in a simple braid, weathered skin, a sewn fur parka, a necklace of pierced fox teeth and small shells. Usually working: scraping hides, grinding roots, sewing.
THE CAMP — an open-air winter camp of five low round huts of hides on a gentle slope above a frozen river, edges weighted with mammoth bones, tusks and stones, a hearth inside each hut, a long limestone ridge behind. No caves.
THE VENUS — a small dark-grey fired clay figurine about the height of a hand, a rounded abstract female form, faceless head with two small holes, always shown as a museum object at a respectful distance.
People wear fitted sewn fur clothing, hoods and boots (never loincloths). Tools are Upper Paleolithic: flint blades, bone needles with eyes, antler picks, spears with stone points, nets of twisted plant fibre. Woolly mammoths have small ears. Scientists, archaeologists and modern people are generic figures seen from behind or with faces out of frame.

Hard rules: 16:9, no text or letters or numbers in the image, no watermarks, no logos, no signs, no flags, no blood, no wounds, no fighting, no hunting contact, no nudity, no cartoon or stick-figure style, no "cave man with a club", no recognisable real people. Hands have five fingers; count people exactly as described. Screens, papers and charts are always blurred and unreadable. Graves are shown with respect, ornaments and ochre in focus, never as gore.
