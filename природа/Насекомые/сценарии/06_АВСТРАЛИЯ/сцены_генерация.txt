PROJECT CONTEXT:
Project: a 13-minute English YouTube nature documentary for adults, channel "Insects" of the Animals network, titled "10 Bugs in Australia So Dangerous Even Experts Keep Their Distance". Story: ten creatures of Australia ranked by how seriously experts take them: the huntsman spider (scary, almost harmless), burrowing scorpions that glow under ultraviolet light, processionary caterpillars whose hairs cause horse abortions, the white-tailed spider (a myth taken apart), the mouse spider (the funnel-web lookalike), the redback, the Tasmanian jack jumper ant with its own hospital program, the Sydney funnel-web (record male "Hercules"), the honeybee (the real body count) and, at the top, the paralysis tick, which can paralyse and can cause a dangerous allergy to red meat. Second line: is Australia really the most dangerous bug country on Earth? Outback, bushland, suburban homes and gardens, cars, hospitals and laboratories in calm light. Respectful, never horror.

Visual style (used for most images): photorealistic macro nature photography, shallow depth of field, natural soft light. Spiders: crisp detail of eight eyes in groups and fine leg hairs. Scorpions: claws, tail segments, leg hairs. Paralysis tick: body texture and leg hairs, no visible eyes. No compound eyes, no wings, no antennae on any arachnid. Insects: six legs, two antennae, correct wings. Diagrams, maps and comparisons: vintage entomology plate illustration in ink and watercolor on cream paper.

Recurring characters (keep identical across shots; each has a reference image R1–R10):
HUNTSMAN (R1) — flat light-brown hairy spider, eight long banded legs spread sideways, leg span wider than an adult hand, body as long as a thumbnail.
SCORPION (R2) — Australian burrowing scorpion, reddish-brown, heavy claws, curled tail, eight walking legs, as long as a finger.
CATERPILLAR (R3) — processionary caterpillar of the bag shelter moth, dark grey-brown, long pale hairs, as long as a little finger.
WHITE-TAIL (R4) — slim dark grey spider with a pale spot at the tip of the abdomen, as long as a fingernail.
MOUSE SPIDER (R5) — stocky glossy black, bulky jaws, male with a red head, size of a large coin.
REDBACK (R6) — pea-sized glossy black abdomen with a red stripe on the back.
JACK JUMPER (R7) — black ant with orange-yellow jaws and leg tips, large eyes, as long as a fingernail.
FUNNEL-WEB (R8) — male Sydney funnel-web, glossy blue-black, large downward fangs, leg span as wide as a credit card is long.
HONEYBEE (R9) — European honeybee worker, golden-brown with dark bands, smaller than a thumbnail, four wings.
TICK (R10) — Australian paralysis tick, unfed the size of a sesame seed, swells to the size of a fingernail after feeding, eight legs.

Hard rules: 16:9, no text or letters or numbers in the image, no watermarks, no logos, no signs, no labels on diagrams or maps, no recognisable real people (people only as hands, arms, feet, the back of a neck or silhouettes from behind). Anatomy: every spider, scorpion and tick has exactly eight legs and no antennae; every insect six legs and two antennae; bees four wings, ants without wings. No blood, no open wounds, no crushed animals, no fangs in a human face, no children shown. Phobia rule: no large spider or scorpion close-up in scenes S001–S006 (the first forty seconds) — only landscapes, gardens, objects and distant tiny shapes.
