PROJECT CONTEXT:
Project: an 18-minute English YouTube documentary for adults, channel "Sea Creatures" (ocean animals network), titled "Sperm Whales Have Family Names. We Just Learned to Hear Them". Story: off the Caribbean island of Dominica, scientists have followed the same sperm whale families since 2005. The whales live in matriarchal families, hunt squid up to two kilometers deep with the loudest clicks of any animal, and at the surface trade short rhythmic click patterns called codas. Each vocal clan has its own identity rhythm, like a family name; Project CETI found that codas are built from parts (rhythm, tempo, stretching, an extra click) and even vowel-like sounds. In July 2023 the team filmed a sperm whale birth in which unrelated females helped lift the newborn. The video ends with ship noise, the shrinking population and the world's first sperm whale reserve. Calm blue Caribbean water near the surface, black depths, research boat, drones, hydrophones.

Visual style (used for every image): dark cinematic underwater photorealism, deep navy and black water, shafts of light from above, natural bioluminescence where it exists in nature, documentary wildlife photography. Surface scenes: dark cinematic ocean photorealism with natural daylight or moody evening light. Scale diagrams: clean scientific illustration on dark navy background without labels.

Recurring characters (keep identical across shots):
FEMALE SPERM WHALE — an adult female sperm whale of the Dominica families, about eleven meters long, dark grey-brown wrinkled skin, a huge blunt box-shaped head about a third of the body, a narrow underslung lower jaw with a pale lining, a small eye low on the side of the head, a single blowhole at the front left of the head, a low rounded dorsal hump followed by a row of knuckle-like bumps, broad triangular tail flukes with small nicks on the trailing edge, pale scars on the head.
CALF — a young sperm whale about four meters long, lighter grey, wrinkled skin, rounder smaller head, same body plan.
THE RESEARCH BOAT — a small white research boat about twelve meters long with a low cabin and a black hydrophone cable over the side, no name, no flag, no logos.

Hard rules: 16:9, no text or letters or numbers in the image, no watermarks, no logos, no boat names, no flags with symbols, no blood, no wounds, no animals being torn apart, no recognisable real people. People appear only as silhouettes, from behind or in diving gear with faces hidden. Below 200 meters light comes only from torches or bioluminescence. Phone screens, newspapers, journals, notebooks and screens are always blurred and unreadable. Sperm whales always have realistic anatomy: one blowhole on the front left, a low dorsal hump, two small pectoral fins, a horizontal tail fluke, a narrow lower jaw; never a toothy mouth like a shark.
