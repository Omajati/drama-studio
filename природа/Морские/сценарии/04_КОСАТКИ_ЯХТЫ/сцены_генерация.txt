PROJECT CONTEXT:
Project: an 18-minute English YouTube documentary for adults, channel "Sea Creatures" (ocean animals network), titled "One Orca Started Breaking Boats. Now Her Whole Pod Does It". Story: since 2020 a small, critically endangered population of orcas off Spain and Portugal (about 40 animals that hunt Atlantic bluefin tuna) has been grabbing and breaking the rudders of sailboats. Researchers linked the first encounters to an adult female called White Gladis and two juveniles; the behavior spread mostly among young orcas, around nine boats have sunk, yet no person has been hurt by the orcas. The video compares it to the 1987 "salmon hat" fad of orcas near Seattle and explains why wild orcas leave people alone. Night sea, deep blue water, tuna, sailboats, moonlight and bioluminescence.

Visual style (used for every image): dark cinematic underwater photorealism, deep navy and black water, shafts of light from above, natural bioluminescence where it exists in nature, documentary wildlife photography. Surface scenes: dark cinematic ocean photorealism with moonlight or overcast light. Scale diagrams: clean scientific illustration on dark navy background without labels.

Recurring characters (keep identical across shots):
WHITE GLADIS — an adult female orca of the Iberian population, about six meters long, stocky, glossy black with crisp white eye patch, white belly and a light grey saddle patch behind a curved, medium-height dorsal fin with one small notch near its tip.
JUVENILES — young orcas of the same population, about four meters long, slimmer, with short curved dorsal fins, same black-and-white pattern, playful.
THE SAILBOAT — a white twelve-meter cruising sloop with a single white spade rudder, navy stripe on the hull, no name, no flag, no logos.

Hard rules: 16:9, no text or letters or numbers in the image, no watermarks, no logos, no boat names, no flags, no blood, no wounds, no animals being torn apart, no recognisable real people. People appear only as silhouettes, from behind or in diving gear with faces hidden. Below 200 meters light comes only from torches or bioluminescence. Phone screens, newspapers and documents are always blurred and unreadable. Orcas always have realistic anatomy: one dorsal fin, two pectoral fins, horizontal tail fluke.
