PROJECT CONTEXT:
Project: a 17-minute English YouTube documentary for adults, channel "Sea Creatures" (ocean animals network), titled "The Shark That Might Be Older Than Isaac Newton". Story: the Greenland shark lives in near-freezing Arctic and North Atlantic water, swims slower than a person walks and usually carries a pale parasitic copepod on each eye. In 2016 Danish scientists dated the centers of shark eye lenses and estimated the biggest female, about five meters long, at roughly 392 years old, give or take 120 years, so she may have been born before Isaac Newton. The video covers its slow hunting of seals, the parasite and its surprisingly healthy retina, its genome and aging heart, the Icelandic dish hákarl, bycatch, a sleeper shark caught off a coral atoll in Belize in 2022, and a Greenland shark stranded in Ireland in 2026. It ends with a teaser for the next video about walruses. Cold blue-green and black water, sea ice ceilings, Arctic fjords, research boats, laboratories.

Visual style (used for every image): dark cinematic underwater photorealism, deep navy and black water, shafts of light from above, natural bioluminescence where it exists in nature, documentary wildlife photography. Surface and shore scenes: dark cinematic ocean photorealism with cold natural Arctic light. Laboratory and historical scenes: dark cinematic documentary photorealism, muted colors. Scale diagrams: clean scientific illustration on dark navy background without labels.

Recurring characters (keep identical across shots):
OLD GREENLAND SHARK — a huge old female Greenland shark about five meters long, thick heavy cigar-shaped body, rough grey-brown skin with faint darker mottling and pale old scars, short rounded blunt snout, very small eyes with a pale cloudy cornea, a pale ribbon-like parasitic copepod about the length of a paper clip hanging from the center of each eye, two small low dorsal fins set far back, short rounded pectoral fins, no anal fin, a long asymmetric tail with a bigger upper lobe, small mouth with no visible teeth when closed.
THE RESEARCH BOAT — a small grey steel Arctic research boat about fifteen meters long with a low wheelhouse and a winch at the stern, no name, no flag, no logos.

Hard rules: 16:9, no text or letters or numbers in the image, no watermarks, no logos, no boat names, no flags with symbols, no blood, no wounds, no animals being torn apart, no recognisable real people. People appear only as silhouettes, from behind or in diving gear with faces hidden; historical figures never appear, only their era and objects. Below 200 meters light comes only from torches or bioluminescence. Phone screens, magazines, books, papers and screens are always blurred and unreadable. Greenland sharks always have realistic anatomy: small eyes, blunt snout, small low dorsal fins, no fearsome toothy grin, slow heavy body; never a great white shark shape. Walruses have realistic anatomy: two long ivory tusks, thick whiskers, wrinkled brown skin.
