PROJECT CONTEXT:
Project: a 20-minute English-language wildlife documentary for adults, channel of land animals, titled "Two Lions Stopped a Railway. One Forest Still Hunts People". Story: in 1898 two maneless male lions raided the tent camps of workers building the Uganda Railway bridge over the Tsavo River in what is now Kenya, stopping construction for about three weeks, until the engineer shot them in December 1898; their skins are now stuffed in a museum in Chicago, and modern science (tooth damage, bone chemistry, hairs stuck in broken teeth) recounts the legend. Second story: the Sundarbans mangrove delta of India and Bangladesh, where Bengal tigers still take fishermen, crab collectors and honey collectors; tides, brackish water, back-to-front face masks, electrified dummies, nylon net fences, the forest goddess Bonbibi.

Visual style (used for every image): cinematic wildlife documentary photograph, natural light, earthy muted palette with one warm accent (firelight, lantern, golden hour). Historical scenes of 1898 look like archival sepia photographs with period clothing. Size comparisons and maps are naturalist engraving-style illustrations on warm parchment with one human silhouette for scale.

Recurring characters (keep identical across shots):
LION ONE — a big maneless male lion, short sparse ginger ruff instead of a mane, heavy body, a scar on the muzzle, a broken lower canine visible only when the mouth is open.
LION TWO — a second maneless male lion of the same size, slightly paler coat, no scars.
TIGER — a large Bengal tiger, bright orange coat with dense black stripes, white chest, mud on the legs from the mangrove flats.
ENGINEER — a British railway engineer of the 1890s in his thirties, khaki jacket, pith helmet, moustache, rifle; never a recognisable real face.

Hard rules: 16:9, no text or letters or numbers in the image, no watermarks, no logos, no signs, no flags. No blood, no wounds, no bodies, no animals fighting or killing in close-up: attacks are shown only before or after (an empty mat, a dropped lantern, pugmarks, a torn tent flap). Animals with true-to-species anatomy, never cartoonish. People are workers of the 1890s and villagers of the Sundarbans shown with dignity, no recognisable real people. Books, ledgers, notes and photographs are always blurred and unreadable.
