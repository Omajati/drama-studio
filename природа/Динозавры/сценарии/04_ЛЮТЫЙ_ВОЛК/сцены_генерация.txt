PROJECT CONTEXT:
Project: a 17-minute English science documentary for adults on the YouTube channel about dinosaurs and the prehistoric world, titled "Scientists Brought Back the Dire Wolf. Many Say It Isn't One". Story: in April 2025 the company Colossal Biosciences announced three pale "dire wolves" (two males born October 2024, one female born January 2025), made by editing 20 places in 14 genes of gray wolf cells, cloning them and using domestic dogs as surrogate mothers. The video follows the real Ice Age dire wolf (La Brea Tar Pits, horses, bison, ground sloths), the 2021 Nature study that placed it on a separate branch of the dog family about 5.7 million years ago, the 2025 genome preprint that drew the tree differently, the critics (IUCN Canid Specialist Group), and the animals today (2026): grown, about 60 kg, living as a pack on a fenced preserve. Ends on the fossil tooth from Ohio and a teaser about the Tasmanian tiger.

Visual style (used for every image): painterly realism paleoart for Ice Age scenes; painterly realism wildlife art for the living pale wolves; documentary photograph look for museum, lab and field scenes; clean flat scientific illustration for size comparisons and family trees.

Recurring characters (keep identical across shots):
PALE WOLVES — three young adult wolves with very thick creamy-white fur, broad heads, heavy chests, amber eyes; they look like large, sturdy gray wolves with white coats, not giant and not cartoonish.
DIRE WOLF — Ice Age Aenocyon dirus: about the length of a large gray wolf but stockier, broad skull, deep heavy jaws, large teeth hidden behind closed lips, thick tawny to grey-brown fur, shorter legs relative to the body.
FOSSIL TOOTH — a single worn dark-brown fossil canid tooth with cracks.
Scientists and keepers are generic figures in lab coats, scrubs or khaki, seen from behind or with faces out of frame.

Hard rules: 16:9, no text or letters or numbers in the image, no watermarks, no logos, no company names, no magazine titles, no signs, no flags, no blood, no wounds, no fighting, no cartoon style, no recognisable real people. Screens, papers, magazine covers and diagrams are always blurred and unreadable. Dire wolves are never drawn as giant monsters. Animal welfare: wolves look healthy and calm.
