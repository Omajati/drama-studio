# Coal Miners Dug Into a Mountain in Canada — They Exposed a Wall of 10,000 Dinosaur Tracks · 199K просм. · 15.5 мин · Ancient Canada TV · 2026-10-05 · 1L06VW84xh8
Теги: ancient canada, canada ancient history, history documentary, canadian mysteries, ancient history canada
Ancient Canada reveals one of the most extraordinary dinosaur tracksites ever exposed: a massive wall of rock near Grande Cache, Alberta, covered with thousands of footprints left behind roughly 90 million years ago.

The tracks were uncovered as open-pit coal mining cut into the mountains north of Grande Cache. Instead of dinosaur skeletons, miners exposed enormous slabs of ancient rock covered in trackways — surfaces that had once been flat, muddy ground during the Early Cretaceous.

More than 20 tracksites have been identified across the former Smoky River Coal Mine, and researchers estimate that well over 10,000 individual footprints are preserved there. At one site alone, known as W3 Main, approximately 10,000 tracks have been recorded.

The footprints belong to several different kinds of animals. Many were made by heavily armoured nodosaurid dinosaurs, while others came from large and small theropods and ancient birds. Some surfaces preserve long trackways showing animals walking repeatedly across the same muddy landscape.

Today those ancient walking surfaces no longer lie flat. Geological forces tilted the rock layers until some of the track-bearing surfaces now stand at angles of roughly 40 to 60 degrees, creating enormous walls covered in dinosaur footprints.

These tracks preserve something bones rarely can: movement. They show where dinosaurs walked, how they crossed the landscape, which animals shared the same ground, and how heavily some parts of the ancient environment were used.

# A Teacher Followed Bones Up a Canadian Creek — He Found a Herd of Thousands of Dinosaurs · 118K просм. · 15.3 мин · Ancient Canada TV · 2026-10-13 · y8v63KMPueU
Теги: ancient canada, ancient canada tv, canada ancient history, history documentary, canadian mysteries, ancient history canada
Ancient Canada reveals one of the largest dinosaur bonebeds ever discovered in North America: a 73-million-year-old mass grave in Alberta containing the remains of a horned dinosaur herd that may have numbered in the thousands.

The discovery began in the early 1970s when science teacher Al Lakusta noticed large fossil bone fragments scattered along Pipestone Creek near Grande Prairie. Instead of simply collecting them, he followed the pieces upstream and uphill until he found the dark rock layer they were eroding from.

When paleontologists eventually excavated the site, they discovered an extraordinary concentration of dinosaur bones. In some places, more than 100 bones are packed into a single square metre, with almost all of them belonging to the same species. Babies, juveniles and massive adults were all mixed together.

The animal was later named Pachyrhinosaurus lakustai in honour of Lakusta. It was a roughly five-metre-long, two-tonne horned dinosaur related to Triceratops, but instead of a large nose horn it carried a massive bony boss on its snout and distinctive horns and spikes around its frill.

The evidence suggests that an enormous herd died during a catastrophic flooding event. The animals occur together in a single layer, their bones were moved and sorted by water, and individuals of many different ages are preserved together. Researchers believe thousands of Pachyrhinosaurus may have been travelling as a herd when disaster struck.

The scale of the site is still difficult to comprehend. Around 8,000 bones have been excavated from an area only about the size of a tennis court, while most of the bonebed remains buried inside the hill. Estimates of thousands of dinosaurs are based on the incredible density of fossils and the size of the unexcavated deposit.

In this video, we explore how a teacher following a trail of bones discovered one of Canada's greatest dinosaur sites, what may have killed an enormous herd in a single event, and why scientists think these northern dinosaurs may have migrated across prehistoric Alberta in massive groups.

# Scientists Found a Raptor in Canada With Almost Every Bone in Place — What It Showed Is Shocking · 112K просм. · 15.6 мин · Ancient Canada TV · 2026-10-03 · moREDFgJANU

Ancient Canada reveals one of the most extraordinary raptor discoveries ever made in North America: a nearly complete 76-million-year-old skeleton found in Alberta with almost every bone still lying in the position it held when the animal died.

The fossil was discovered in 2014 in Dinosaur Provincial Park and belonged to Saurornitholestes langstoni, a small feathered predator related to Velociraptor. Unlike the scattered teeth and isolated bones that usually represent these dinosaurs, this specimen preserved almost the entire animal, missing only the end of the tail.

That level of preservation gave scientists something they had never really had before: a complete look at the anatomy of a North American raptor.

The skull revealed that Saurornitholestes was not simply a local version of Velociraptor. Its head was shorter and deeper, and detailed analysis showed that North American raptors belonged to a separate evolutionary lineage from the famous Asian forms.

But one of the strangest discoveries was hiding in the front of its upper jaw.

For more than a century, paleontologists had collected unusual ridged teeth known as Zapsalis without knowing exactly what animal they belonged to. In this skeleton, one of those teeth was finally found still sitting in its original position in the jaw, solving a mystery that had existed in museum collections for generations.

Its shape and wear also suggested something unexpected: researchers proposed that this specialised tooth may have been used to preen and maintain feathers, much like modern birds use their beaks.

# A Miner Found a 57,000-Year-Old Wolf in Canada's Ice — Everything Survived Except Its Eyes · 686 просм. · 16.2 мин · Ancient Canada TV · 2026-09-29 · g3HuQimEA2k
Теги: ancient canada, canada ancient history, canada history
Ancient Canada reveals one of the most extraordinary Ice Age discoveries ever made in the Yukon: a 57,000-year-old wolf pup preserved in permafrost with its fur, skin, ears, nose, whiskers and claws still intact.

The discovery happened in 2016 in the Klondike goldfields, when a miner washing away frozen sediment exposed the nearly complete body of a young wolf. Named Zhur by the local First Nation, she is considered the most complete wolf mummy ever discovered. Almost everything survived — except her eyes.

Researchers used X-rays, tooth development, ancient DNA and chemical analysis to reconstruct parts of her short life. They determined that Zhur was only around six to seven weeks old when she died, still young enough to depend heavily on her mother and the rest of the pack.

Her preservation was possible because she appears to have been buried extremely quickly, probably when the den around her collapsed. The sudden burial protected the body from scavengers and oxygen, while the frozen Yukon ground preserved her soft tissues for tens of thousands of years.

But one of the biggest surprises came from her diet. Chemical signatures in her body indicated that her mother had been feeding heavily on aquatic resources, most likely salmon. Instead of surviving entirely on mammoths, bison, horses and other Ice Age mammals, this ancient wolf family may have relied heavily on fish during the summer.

Ancient DNA also connected Zhur to northern wolf populations from Siberia, Alaska and northern North America, placing her close to the ancestry of wolves that still survive today.

# A 21-Metre Sea Monster Was Found on a Canadian Riverbank — It Took Helicopters to Get It Out · 37K просм. · 15.5 мин · Ancient Canada TV · 2026-10-21 · mzWuOjnSxRc
Теги: ancient canada, ancient canada tv, canada ancient history, history documentary, ancient history canada, canadian history, hidden canada
Ancient Canada reveals one of the largest marine reptiles ever recovered: a 21-metre ichthyosaur discovered along a remote riverbank in northeastern British Columbia.

The first bones were noticed in the early 1990s along the Sikanni Chief River, where a layer of Triassic rock appears for only a few weeks each summer when the water level drops. Ribs, vertebrae and pieces of skull stretched along the bank, all belonging to a single enormous animal.

When the skeleton was finally prepared and measured, it reached about 21 metres from snout to tail. Its skull alone was more than five metres long, making it the largest marine reptile known from a substantial skeleton ever recovered.

But excavating it became an enormous engineering challenge. There was no road to the site, the fossil was underwater for most of the year, and the surrounding rock was too hard for ordinary hand tools. Helicopters had to carry compressors, jackhammers, rock saws, crews and supplies into the wilderness.

The skeleton was eventually cut from the riverbank in massive protected blocks. The largest block contained part of the skull and weighed about 4.5 tonnes — almost the maximum load the helicopter could lift. The excavation took three field seasons, followed by more than four years of laboratory preparation.

The animal was an enormous ichthyosaur that lived more than 200 million years ago, when an ancient ocean covered what is now northeastern British Columbia. Unlike many familiar ichthyosaurs, its massive jaws had no teeth. Scientists still debate exactly how an animal this large fed, with ideas ranging from swallowing small soft-bodied prey whole to more specialised feeding strategies.

The project was led by marine reptile specialist Elizabeth “Betsy” Nicholls, whose determination turned what initially seemed like an impossible excavation into one of the most ambitious fossil recoveries ever attempted. Tragically, she died only two months before the scientific paper naming the animal was published.

In this video, we explore how a 21-metre sea reptile ended up exposed on a Canadian riverbank, why helicopters and heavy machinery were needed to recover it, what scientists learned from its enormous toothless skull, and how one paleontologist spent years making sure the river did not wash the giant away forever.
