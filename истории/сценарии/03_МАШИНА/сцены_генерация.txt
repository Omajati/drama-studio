PROJECT CONTEXT:
Project: a 30-minute Russian-language YouTube documentary for adults, channel «Истории RU» («Как жили в СССР»), titled "Как работали в СССР? Суд за ОПОЗДАНИЕ, норма по секундомеру и ТРИНАДЦАТАЯ зарплата". Story: how ordinary people worked in the USSR, told through one family. YOU — a village boy who comes to a factory construction site in 1930 at 18, learns his trade in three months on courses of the Central Institute of Labour (workers trained to strike a chisel with a hammer by an instructor's stopwatch), lives through rate-setters, Stakhanov's record of 1935, labour books (1939) and the decree of June 1940 that made being twenty minutes late a court matter. The institute's director, a poet turned engineer, is arrested in 1938 and shot in 1939. THE SON — a lathe operator in 1975 Kuibyshev: five-day week, the "thirteenth salary", board of honour, potato fields, payday queues, free milk for hazardous work, holiday food parcels. Then and now: working hours, vacation, retirement, control by stopwatch versus apps.

Visual style (used for every image): photorealistic cinematic documentary stills; 1920s–1940s in muted colours with warm lamp light and film grain; 1960s–1970s in warm natural colours; cool modern documentary photos for the present day. Interiors, clothes, cars and objects strictly of their decade.

Recurring characters (keep identical across shots):
YOU — a Russian village-born man: at 18–20 (1930–1932) lean, short fair hair, broad face, grey eyes, flat cap, worn jacket over a collarless shirt; at 23–28 (1935–1940) the same face, short fair moustache, cap, dark work jacket or overalls.
THE SON — at 30 (1975) the same fair hair and grey eyes as his father, 1970s haircut, clean-shaven, blue work overalls or a knitted sweater.
THE DIRECTOR — a man of about 40–55 in the 1920s–1930s, short dark hair combed back, clean-shaven round face, attentive eyes, dark jacket and tie (period appearance only, not a portrait of a real person).
THE INSTRUCTOR — a man of about 35 in a 1920s dark work coat and cap, a brass stopwatch on a chain.
THE MINER — a broad-shouldered miner of about 30 in a 1930s miner's helmet with a lamp, coal-dusted face.
VIEWER — a person in their 30s, casual clothes, modern apartment.

Hard rules: 16:9, no text or letters or numbers in the image, no watermarks, no logos, no shop signs, no flags, no political symbols or portraits of leaders, no slogans, no license plates, no violence, no blood, no weapons, no recognisable real people. Documents, labour books, newspapers, memos, ledgers, notices, case files and phone or laptop screens are always blurred and unreadable. The arrest is shown only through doors, empty rooms and light. Recurring motifs: the grey-blue labour book, the stopwatch, the hammer and chisel, the clock above the factory gate.
